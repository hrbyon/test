The cell, and the circuit it is

The electrodes keep the same positions in both halves, so the resistor between WE and RE is the slice of solution between them. Its width follows Ru/Rtotal, not the distance — a resistor symbol stands for resistance, and Ru saturates while distance does not.

Two electrodes. One electrode is both the reference and the current carrier, so the meter sees the WE interface, the whole solution resistance, and the CE interface in series — and the CE interface moves as it works. Nothing in the reading belongs to the WE alone. It is still the right circuit when no current flows: a pH meter, a potentiometric titration, an open-circuit measurement.

Three electrodes. The two jobs are split. Current flows only in the WE–CE loop; the RE hangs on a voltmeter input drawing essentially nothing, so its interface is never disturbed. What survives is Ru — the one term the third electrode cannot remove, only shrink.

What each electrode is for

click one to pick it out of the drawing
WE

Working

The electrode you care about. The reaction under study happens here, and it is deliberately the limiting one.

RE

Reference

Holds a fixed, known potential. Almost no current passes through it, so its equilibrium — and therefore its potential — is never disturbed.

CE

Counter

Completes the circuit and carries the whole current. Its reaction must never run out, so it is made large.
